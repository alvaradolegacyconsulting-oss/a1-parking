# Visitor Passes

Visitor passes let a guest's vehicle park at your property without being towed. This guide covers how passes work, who can issue them, and the limits that apply.

Visitor passes are part of **PM Starter**, **PM Pro** and **Operator Pro**.

## When visitor passes are needed

- **Resident guests**: friends or family visiting overnight or for a weekend
- **Service providers**: HVAC, plumbers, exterminators, cleaners
- **Events**: a party or gathering at a resident's home
- **Real estate showings**
- **Move-in and move-out help**
- **A resident's temporary car**: a rental while their own car is in the shop

Without a pass, a vehicle that isn't registered at the property can be cited or towed.

---

## Two ways to get a pass

### 1. The guest scans the property's visitor QR code

Each property has a **visitor QR code** (and link) that the office posts on signage. The guest scans it, enters their plate, the unit they're visiting, and optionally their name and a vehicle description, and gets a **24-hour pass** right away. No login or app is needed.

The confirmation screen shows the pass's real **issued** and **valid until** times. The guest should screenshot it.

### 2. A resident issues it from the resident portal

Residents with an account can issue passes for their guests from the **Visitor Passes** section of their portal. The resident is responsible for the passes they issue.

A guest who can't scan the code — no phone, or a dead battery — can be helped by the resident they're visiting, from the resident portal.

---

## Renewing a pass

A pass lasts until its **valid until** time. To keep parking after that, the guest (or resident) requests a new pass the same way.

- **A renewal creates a new pass**, starting when it's requested and running for the full duration. It doesn't extend the old one.
- **Renewing early still counts as a new pass.** A guest who renews 4 hours before expiry has used two passes.
- **Tapping submit twice by accident is fine.** An identical request within about 2 minutes returns the same pass and doesn't count again.
- Drivers see the pass that's valid the longest, so overlapping passes never make a guest look uncovered.

---

## Visitor pass limits

Each property can set a **visitor pass limit**: the number of passes the **same plate** can receive at that property in any **rolling 30 days**.

- **Every pass counts**, including renewals, and including a pass that was later revoked because the plate was prohibited (see below). Counting revoked passes stops a prohibit-then-reissue loop from clearing the count.
- **When a plate reaches the limit,** the request is refused. The guest sees a message to contact the property office, plus the real expiry of any pass they still hold.
- **No limit is set by default.** A blank limit means unlimited.

**Choosing a number.** Set it well above normal visiting and low enough to catch someone parking daily. A car visiting a few times a week is a guest; a car there almost every day is using visitor passes in place of registration.

**Where to set it:** the manager portal → the property's **Settings** tab.

### Exempt plates

For legitimate frequent visitors (caregivers, a family member who visits weekly, a regular contractor), add the plate to the property's **exempt list** (manager portal → Settings → **Visitor Pass Quota Exemptions**). An exempt plate isn't counted against the limit.

**An exemption does not protect the vehicle from being towed.** It only stops the plate being counted against the limit. The vehicle still needs a pass, or to be a registered resident vehicle, exactly as before. That is also how the setting is labelled in the manager portal, and the wording matters: the list used to be called "Exempt Plates", which led property owners to believe the vehicles on it were protected. See [Adding Properties](04-adding-properties.md).

### Plates not permitted at a property

The property can mark a plate as **not permitted**. A plate on that list can't get a visitor pass there, and any passes it already holds are revoked.

**This is the only way a pass gets revoked.** There is no "revoke this pass" action — a pass otherwise runs to its valid-until time. If a plate shouldn't be here, prohibiting it is the action, and it clears the passes it holds.

## Reviewing passes

**Manager portal → Visitor Passes** shows the active passes at your property, recently expired passes, and each pass's details (plate, unit, times, who issued it).

**Resident portal → Visitor Passes** shows only the passes that resident issued.

---

## When passes expire

When a pass reaches its valid-until time, the vehicle is no longer covered. The pass stays in the records for audit, and it still counts toward the 30-day limit.

---

## Common questions

**A guest says they had a pass but the vehicle was cited. What happened?**
Check the pass in the manager portal:
1. Had it expired before the citation time?
2. Was the plate entered correctly?
3. Was it issued for the right property?

The records show which passes existed, and when, at the time of the citation.

**A guest renewed but was refused. Why?**
Their plate has reached the property's visitor pass limit for the last 30 days. The office can raise the limit or add the plate to the exempt list.

**Can a resident issue a pass for their own rental car?**
Yes. Passes are for a plate, not a person.

**Can a pass be issued retroactively?**
No. Passes are forward-looking.

**How do we handle a big one-time event?**
Tell your towing partner the event timing ahead of time, issue passes for known attendees, and if guests would hit the limit, raise it or add their plates to the exempt list for the event.

---

## Best practices

- **Post the visitor QR code** where guests park, and tell residents about it.
- **Set a limit only when you see a pattern** worth limiting, and pair it with the exempt list for legitimate regulars.
- **Tell guests to screenshot their confirmation.** It shows the real valid-until time.
